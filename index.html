Chord Timing Tester

How it works: Enter a word and the chord you use to type it, then start the test. Each round the word appears on screen — type it using your chord while the app measures exactly how long you hold the chord (from the first key pressed to the last key released). Repeat as many times as you like, then save the session to track your progress or compare multiple chords for the same word. Don't worry about making mistakes, they are not taken into account in the stats.
1 What to test

Enter the word you want to practice and a label for the chord shortcut you'll use (e.g. t+h+e). The chord label is just for your reference as an idicator for the records — the app detects what your device actually outputs. So it has no tractical effect. You can also call it "test1" if you like. Instead of manually entering the word and chord, you can use Load from Library → to fill both fields automatically from your synced chord list.

2 Lead-in Words optional

In real typing you always come from some previous word before you type the next one. Lead-in words simulate this: each test round starts with a lead-in word you type first — immediately followed by the target word. This makes timing more realistic and lets you measure the transition pause (how many milliseconds you wait between finishing the lead-in chord and pressing the first key of the target chord). When multiple lead-in words are listed, one is chosen at random each round so no two rounds feel identical. Instead of pressing the "add" button, you can also just type a word and press Enter.
Leave empty to test the target word in complete isolation.

Results

All saved sessions are stored locally in your browser. Click any row to expand a timing chart. Tick multiple sessions and use Compare selected to view them side by side. You can click on separate entries to view their charts and check on the progression during that session.

Chord Library

What is the Library? Your Library holds all word→chord mappings from your CharaChorder device. Load it once via USB or JSON file, then use Load from Library → in the Test tab to instantly fill in any word and its chord, or use From Library… in step 2 above to pick lead-in words for more realistic practice.
